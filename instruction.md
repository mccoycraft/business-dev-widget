# BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS CALCULATOR

## DETAILED GUIDE FOR ACCOUNTANTS & USERS

Dear Precious,

This is a simple but powerful online tool to calculate when a business will start making profit. Everything is in **Nigerian Naira (₦)**.

Below is a very detailed explanation of every part of the page so you can use it confidently and also explain it to clients.

### 1. MAIN PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL
This calculator answers the most important question in business:
**"How many products/services do I need to sell before I stop losing money and start making profit?"**

### 2. TOP SECTION – INTRODUCTION
**Title:** Break-Even Analysis  
**Description:** "Determine the exact number of units you need to sell to cover costs and start making profit in Naira."

This is just an introduction. Nothing to change here.

### 3. KEY FINANCIAL CONCEPTS
This box explains the three most important terms:

- **Fixed Costs**: Money you must pay every month no matter how much you sell.  
  Examples: Shop rent, staff salaries, electricity, internet, insurance.

- **Variable Costs**: Money spent to produce or buy each single item.  
  Examples: Raw materials, packaging, transportation per unit.

- **Selling Price**: How much the customer pays for one unit.

### 4. SUMMARY CARDS (The 4 Important Numbers)

1. **Startup Investment**  
   Total money the owner put into the business at the beginning.

2. **Contribution Margin**  
   This is the most important number!  
   Formula: Selling Price – Variable Cost  
   Example: ₦32,000 – ₦12,500 = ₦19,500  
   → This means every unit sold gives you ₦19,500 profit after paying the cost of making it.

3. **Break-even Point**  
   The exact number of units the business must sell to cover all costs.  
   In the example: **24 units**  
   → If you sell less than 24 units → you are losing money.  
   → If you sell exactly 24 units → you have no profit, no loss.  
   → If you sell more than 24 units → you start making profit.

4. **Projected ROI**  
   Return on Investment.  
   It shows what percentage return the business makes on the initial capital based on selling 100 units.

### 5. THE GRAPH (Very Important – Please Read Carefully)

The big chart shows two lines:

- **Blue Line (Revenue)**: Total money coming in from sales.
- **White/Grey Line (Total Cost)**: Fixed costs + variable costs.

**How to read the graph:**

- The point where the two lines cross is the **Break-even Point**.
- There is a vertical dashed line showing the exact break-even unit.
- A blue dot marks the break-even point on the chart.
- To the left of the line → Business is losing money.
- To the right of the line → Business is making profit.

The numbers at the bottom (0, 20, 40, ..., 100) represent the number of units sold.  
The numbers on the left side represent money in Naira.

**Tip:** You can hover your mouse over the lines to see exact values.

### 6. BREAK-EVEN FORMULA SECTION

This shows the mathematical formula used:
**Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price − Variable Cost)**

It also displays the final answer in big bold text (e.g., 24 units).

### 7. THE SLIDERS (This is where you control everything)

You can move these four sliders to test different situations:

1. **Fixed Costs (₦)**  
   Move this to see how rent or salary changes affect the break-even point.

2. **Variable Cost per Unit (₦)**  
   Change this to see the effect of buying cheaper or more expensive materials.

3. **Selling Price per Unit (₦)**  
   Very powerful slider. Increasing this usually lowers the break-even point.

4. **Startup Investment (₦)**  
   Used only for calculating the ROI percentage.

**Important Tip:**  
Every time you move any slider, **all numbers and the graph update immediately**.

### 8. BUSINESS INTERPRETATION (Bottom Section)

This part explains the results in plain English. For example:

"Your business needs to sell at least 24 units to reach the break-even point.  
Every additional unit sold after break-even contributes ₦19,500 directly to profit."

This is very useful when explaining to clients who are not good with numbers.

### 9. PRACTICAL EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Bakery Business**
- Fixed Costs: ₦450,000 (rent + salaries)
- Variable Cost: ₦12,500 (flour, sugar, etc per cake)
- Selling Price: ₦32,000 per cake
  → Needs to sell **24 cakes** to break even.

**Example 2: Phone Accessories Seller**
- Fixed Costs: ₦800,000
- Variable Cost: ₦8,000 per item
- Selling Price: ₦25,000
  → Break-even will be higher or lower depending on the numbers.

### 10. HOW TO USE THIS TOOL EFFECTIVELY

1. Ask the business owner for their real numbers.
2. Enter the numbers using the sliders.
3. Look at the Break-even Point.
4. If the number is too high, discuss with the client how to reduce it:
    - Increase selling price?
    - Reduce cost of materials?
    - Reduce rent or salaries?
5. Use the graph to show them visually.
6. Take screenshot of good results to include in your report.

### 11. WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN FOR DECISION MAKING

- Low break-even point (e.g., 10–30 units) → Business is relatively safe.
- High break-even point (e.g., 100+ units) → Business is risky. Need to review costs or pricing.
- High Contribution Margin → Very good. Business can scale easily.
