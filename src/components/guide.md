# 1. MAIN PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL

The calculator answers a key business question:

**"How many units must a business sell before it covers all costs and starts making profit?"**

This is known as the **Break-even Point**.

At break-even:

Total Revenue = Total Cost

Business is not making a loss

Business has not yet generated profit

Every sale after the break-even point contributes directly toward profit.

# 3. KEY BUSINESS CONCEPTS

This section explains the main financial terms used in the calculation.

---

## Fixed Costs

Fixed costs are expenses that remain the same regardless of how many units are sold.

Examples:

Shop rent

Staff salaries

Utilities

Insurance

Office expenses

Formula:

```

Fixed Costs = Expenses that do not change with production volume

```

---

## Variable Costs

Variable costs increase as more units are produced or sold.

Examples:

Raw materials

Packaging

Production costs

Per-unit delivery costs

Formula:

```

Total Variable Cost = Variable Cost Per Unit × Units Sold

```

---

## Selling Price

Selling price is the amount charged to customers for one unit of a product or service.

Example:

```

Selling Price = ₦35,000 per unit

```

---

## Startup Capital

Startup capital is the initial investment required to launch the business.

Examples:

Equipment purchase

Initial inventory

Business registration

Setup costs

Startup capital is used to estimate the business investment level and calculate projected ROI.

# 4. SUMMARY CARDS (Business Performance Overview)

The calculator displays four important business indicators.

---

## 1. Startup Investment

Shows the amount of money invested to start the business.

Example:

```

₦2,500,000

```

This helps measure the size of the initial business commitment.

---

## 2. Contribution Margin

Contribution margin shows how much money remains from each sale after removing the variable cost.

Formula:

```

Selling Price - Variable Cost Per Unit

```

Example:

```

₦35,000 - ₦14,500 = ₦20,500

```

Meaning:

Every unit sold contributes ₦20,500 toward covering fixed costs and generating profit.

---

## 3. Break-even Point

This shows the number of units required to cover all fixed costs.

Formula:

```

Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin

```

Example:

```

₦650,000 ÷ ₦20,500 = 31.71 units

```

The calculator rounds this upward:

```

32 units required to break even

```

---

## 4. Projected ROI (100 Units Per Month)

The calculator estimates return on investment assuming the business sells:

```

100 units per month

```

Formula used:

```

((Revenue - Fixed Costs - Variable Costs) ÷ Startup Investment) × 100

```

Where:

```

Revenue = 100 × Selling Price

Variable Cost = 100 × Variable Cost Per Unit

```

This provides an estimate of business performance based on monthly sales of 100 units.

# 6. BREAK-EVEN FORMULA

The calculator uses:

```

Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price - Variable Cost Per Unit)

```

Example:

```

₦650,000 ÷ (₦35,000 - ₦14,500)

₦650,000 ÷ ₦20,500

= 31.71 units

Rounded result:

32 units

```

# 9. PRACTICAL BUSINESS EXAMPLE

## Small Manufacturing Business

Assumptions:

```

Fixed Costs: ₦650,000

Variable Cost Per Unit: ₦14,500

Selling Price Per Unit: ₦35,000

Startup Investment: ₦2,500,000

```

Calculation:

```

Contribution Margin:

₦35,000 - ₦14,500

= ₦20,500

```

Break-even:

```

₦650,000 ÷ ₦20,500

= 31.71 units

```

Required sales:

```

32 units

```

The business must sell approximately 32 units before covering operating costs.

# 12. COMMON QUESTIONS

## Q: What happens if I sell more than 100 units?

**Answer:**

The calculation formula works for any sales quantity.

The graph only displays up to 100 units for visualization.

---

## Q: Does startup investment affect break-even?

**Answer:**

No.

Break-even is calculated using:

```

Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin

```

Startup investment is only used for the ROI calculation.

---

## Q: Can I save my calculations?

**Answer:**

The calculator does not automatically save values.

Users should take screenshots or record important figures manually.

---

## Q: What happens if my variable cost is higher than my selling price?

**Answer:**

The business has a negative contribution margin.

This means each sale creates a loss, and pricing or costs should be reviewed.
